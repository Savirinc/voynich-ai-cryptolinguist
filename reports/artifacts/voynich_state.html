Voynich: State of the Evidence

The text is built like a language, yet every proven method to read it has failed and no word in it has ever been tied to a meaning. A real language in an invented script remains the best explanation, not a demonstrated one.

Solid facts

  • Vellum from 1404–1438; five scribes; drawings and text made together on the page.
  • 7,060 distinct words on the working pages; 69% occur only once; 149 words make up half the text.
  • Words are assembled from parts, with about 21–28 endings that attach freely to stems.
  • Spelling is loose in a patterned way, through a few swaps: e/ee, r/l, t/k, and d or q added or dropped.
  • Two varieties (Currier A and B) share one grammar shape.
  • Common words often stand next to themselves (daiin daiin), about ten times more than in European languages.
  • Labels are mostly unique words. They are not numbers, and drawings sharing a label do not look alike.
  • No anchor word has ever held up, for anyone.

Still open

  • A known language written with syllable signs. Vietnamese is the closest match on word repeats.
  • A lost or unrecorded language. Only pictures or anchors could open it.
  • A constructed or invented language (Enochian, Lingua Ignota as controls).
  • Meaningless but sophisticated text. It now has a sharp test.

Next tests

  1. Per-word repeat individuality. Some common words never repeat back to back while others repeat five times more than chance. The best generator cannot copy this, so it separates a real vocabulary from generated text.
  2. Clean-up round. Re-test d, q, e/ee and topic clustering with line position and writing sessions controlled.
  3. Syllable route. Vietnamese through a validated syllable-sign attack.
