# voynich.ninja survey: ILLUSTRATIONS, LABELS and ANCHORS

Compiled 2026-10-24. Read-only; no login, no posting.

**Coverage and limits.** I fetched about 60 threads in full, every page, through the forum's public archive view (`/archive/index.php/thread-N.html`). Search-engine summaries filled in a few more. Since about 2025 the forum shows guests only the opening post and roughly one post per page. Most replies read "Register or login to view the content", and Google Drive or spreadsheet links show as "[LINK]". So the critiques below come from the posts that are visible plus search snippets. Several key resources sit behind a free registration: Knowles' match spreadsheet, Koen's month-name spreadsheet and the label-count spreadsheet. Arham would have to log in to get them. I did not try to get around the restriction.

Legend: **CONFIRMS / CONTRADICTS / EXTENDS / NEW** compares each item with our findings: E055 (pharma drawings that share a label do not look alike), C22 (zodiac labels are not numbers) and C23 (labels echo their own page's text about 1.7x).

- f5r Paris quadrifolia
- f6v Ricinus
- f9v Viola
- f16r Cannabis
- f17v Tamus/Dioscorea
- f18r Calendula
- f18v Malva (JKP)
- f21r Polygonum aviculare / Herniaria
- f23r Veronica
- f25v Plantago
- f26r Sedum telephium (JKP)
- f32r Prunella
- f35v oak
- f43v teasel
- f49r Cuscuta (JKP)
- f65v chamomile

https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2608.html

**Critique.**
- A 2021 thread reports that "the only plant generally agreed-upon is Viola"; poppy, water lily, Ricinus and Cannabis are all disputed. https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3491.html (R. Sale, JKP)
- R. Sale: even with a firm ID, "how to determine which vord(s) to compare with the proposed name… there is no answer". JKP keeps f21r as Polygonum, Peplis, Ammannia or Euphorbia.
- f5r Paris versus Trientalis: Paris has the wrong leaf count. Anton, JKP and Koen G, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-1421.html
- MarcoP and ReneZ warn repeatedly that "looks most like X" is not enough (thread-2209, thread-528).
- Stolfi cites Barschius: the plants "escaped observation here in Germany", and many plants are plainly not real (f5v, f11r, f40r). thread-4067

**Status vs ours: NEW, but weak as an anchor.** No identification has ever been turned into a sign value that survived testing (see §8). The usable output is a short list of pages where a plant name could be pre-registered as a crib: f2r, f2v, f9v, f6v, f16r, f25v.

## 2. Pharma-to-herbal visual matches and their labels (our E055)

**2a. Oocephalus 2016** (thread-196, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-196.html)
- Rare pharma labels that also occur on herbal pages: *opchor*, 6 occurrences in the whole manuscript, is a pharma label and also appears in the text of f13r, but the plants are only vaguely similar.
- *otory*, 4 occurrences, labels an odd leaf on f102v2 and a similar-leaved plant elsewhere.
- *okeoly* is a label that also appears in the text just above it.
- Of Th. Petersen's published pharma-herbal matches: "For those where I could see the resemblance, the labels do not occur on the respective herbal pages."
- Some of Petersen's matches are wrong (item 8, f102).

**2b. MarcoP 2017** ("Matching pharma/small plants labels in context", https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-2013-2.html)
- Of more than 200 pharma labels, only about 4 close matches were found in the page's own paragraph. One of those depends on t=k, one is split over two lines with a different suffix, and two are split into two words.
- Conclusion: "the paragraphs do not usually mention the specific illustrated items."
- Visual comparison of items with identical labels:
  - The *otoldy* group (f89r1, f89r2, f99r and a fourth) has 3 or 4 roots and possibly a jar. Only two of them are "vaguely similar", and one root is "quite different".
  - The *o[kt]ory* group (a leaf, a plant and a container) is only vaguely similar.
  - The two *okary* plants in the top row of f99r are the most similar pair.
- Koen G (2020) explains why labels may not recur in the text: inflection, text that refers to "these ingredients", or meaningless text.

**2c. Anton** (thread-543, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-543.html)
- On f88r 38% of the labels are unique words, and on f88v 50%.
- Of the labels that do recur, 25% (f88r) and 40% (f88v) occur only outside the herbal folios.
- That argues against "pharma labels = herbal plant names".

**2d. Mark Knowles / Koen G / merrimacga, "Matching Plant Images Internally"** (2023 onward, 21 pages, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4067.html)
- Knowles listed about 108 herbal-to-pharma matches by comparing every herbal drawing with every small drawing and keeping the 4 best.
- This became a shared Google Sheet in which each match is rated 0–3 by several raters. Sources merged into it: MK (Knowles), ThP (Th. Petersen), WD (Wladimir D) and Koen. Duplicates were removed.
- Examples: f87v[1,1], a root-and-leaf match; f89r1[3,2], faces in the roots (nablator); MK88 = f100r[1,2] and MK90 = f99r[4,1], which can be chained.
- Stolfi (2025): the herbal page and f102r1 "are not just the same plant. The layout of the branches is the same, the root drawn from the same perspective", which means copying from a shared exemplar.
- Koen: there are about twice as many small plants as large ones, so no neat one-to-one correspondence should be expected.
- R. Sale: matching the "most probable IDs" against medieval herbal monographs was "a total strike out".

**2e. Karlov 2026** (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-6044.html)
- Proposes a pre-registered test. Pick herbal-pharma pairs from the drawings only, then ask whether the pharma label's rare glyph bigrams and trigrams are over-represented on the matched herbal page, compared with other herbal pages.
- A second level uses the whole f102 panel text against the union of its matched herbal pages.
- Controls: Currier A/B, hand, length, and the non-independence of fragments on the same panel.
- Example given: two herbal plants on one bifolium whose pharma fragments sit side by side on f102r2.
- Replies are hidden. A search snippet shows one reply claiming a negative computer-vision comparison of roots, stems and flowers, and "no statistically significant linguistic overlap". That reply was flagged "possibly AI generated" and published no data.

**Status vs ours: CONFIRMS E055.** Two independent earlier observers, MarcoP (otoldy/okory) and Oocephalus (Petersen matches), saw the same thing we did: identical labels do not mean identical drawings, and visually matched drawings do not share labels. **EXTENDS:** the community has never run Karlov's sub-word version, and the rated match sheet is a reusable "ground truth" made without looking at the text.

## 3. Label studies in general (C22, C23)

**3a. "Labelese" characteristics** (Nick Pelling's list, posted by VViews; thread-2862, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2862.html)
- Many labels start with ot- or ok-, and yk- is over-represented.
- Labels are short, often end in -y, and many repeat.
- Almost none start with qo-.
- Strong "paired" structure on f70v2 Pisces: otolal, otaral, otalar, otalam, dolaram, okaram.
- MarcoP: zodiac label TTR 263/298 = 0.882, compared with 0.791 for the 360-angel degree table (Ars Paulina). Similar labels cluster in adjacent signs, e.g. oeeo-/oeeeo- mostly in Libra.
- ReneZ: the label behaviour shows "some intention… not arbitrary filler".
- Pelling: labels like *oty* are too short to be whole words, which suggests abbreviation.

**3b. Label vocabulary versus running text** (search snippets citing MarcoP and Stolfi)
- About 70% of labels match words found in the main corpus.
- "Normal" words make up 88% of label tokens against 96.5% of text tokens. Abnormal words are 12.4% of label tokens against 3.7% of text tokens.
- Labels follow the same in-word rules as running text. david, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-833.html

**3c. Shared labels across nymphs, stars and plants** (danielemlynjones, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3862.html)
- Claim: shared labels show that nymphs personify stars and plants.
- Koen G's reply is the most useful critique. Shared labels may be (i) dates, (ii) list numbers that point to external lists, or (iii) a "reductive" encoding in which different plaintext words collapse to one cipher word.
- RobGea confirms that v101 has "a stackful of labels" on most folios.

**3d. Definition problem** (JKP, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3376.html)
- There is no agreed definition of which text counts as a label (57v rings, Q13 top lines, rosettes). That undermines every label statistic.
- Anton: labels may be "preposition+noun" constructions, and "there may be no plant names in the Herbal folios".

**3e. Zodiac labels starting with o-** (takrobat 2025, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4829.html)
- Overall, 230 of 299 zodiac labels (76.9%) start with o-.
- The share falls by sign: Aries 29/30, Pisces 25, Taurus 24, Cancer 24, Libra 24, Leo 22, Virgo 21, Scorpio 20, Sagittarius 15/30 (with 10 y-initial).
- Aries is the most internally consistent sign.

**Status vs ours.**
- C23 is **partly in tension**. MarcoP found that pharma labels almost never appear verbatim in their own paragraph, about 4 of 200 and those approximate. Our 1.7x is a ratio for exact word types against other pages, which is a different test, and Stolfi's 88% "normal word" figure fits it.
- Both can be true: labels are ordinary words that are over-represented on their own page, yet the specific label rarely appears in the specific paragraph. We should report the absolute rate next to the ratio.
- C22 **CONFIRMED and EXTENDED**. MarcoP's TTR of 0.88 matches our 245 distinct labels out of 292. His "adjacent-sign clustering" is a different and testable structure from "same position across months", which we tested and found absent.
- takrobat's o- gradient is **NEW**. It looks like the same location-in-book drift we found for q- (C19).

## 4. Zodiac section

**Counts** (Koen G, "Counts", https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2455.html)
- 299 nymphs plus 4 central figures (303). Mostly 29–30 per sign; Aries and Taurus are each split into 2×15.
- f68r1 has 29 stars, all labelled. f68r2 has 59 stars: 24 labelled, 12 unlabelled, plus an outer ring of 23 unlabelled stars.
- Q20 margins have 324 stars, 163 of them painted red.
- Star counts do not always match nymph counts (Pisces: 29 nymphs, 31 stars).

**Degrees, not days.** The majority view is that 30 nymphs = 30 degrees: david's myrogenesis / paranatellonta idea (https://voynich.ninja/thread-535.html), MarcoP's 360-angel parallel, and Dobri's Lapidario degree-stars (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4448.html).
- Stolfi's critique: the scribe was "learning to draw nymphs", was bad at dividing circles, squeezed or patched (Pisces at 11:30), and cared more about the star count than the nymph count.
- Koen G and ReneZ: the Aries/Taurus split is a matter of space and layout, not meaning (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4522.html).
- "Labels are numerals 1–30" was proposed by an anonymous poster and Bernd (https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-4536.html). **Our C22 contradicts it.**

**shed-/ched- alternation** (MarcoP 2016, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-580.html)
- In the zodiac ring text there are 15 tokens with the prefixes shed- or ched-, on 7 pages.
- shed- appears only in Libra, Leo and Sagittarius, the masculine (diurnal) signs. ched- appears only in Pisces, Taurus, Cancer and Scorpio, the feminine signs.
- A Sagittarius nymph label *ched…* breaks the pattern, and labels generally don't follow it.
- n is small, and it has never been tested properly. **NEW and testable.**

**Month names (Latin script).**
- They are a later addition in a different hand and ink (Koen G, Zauriek, Stolfi: "only one scribe… written after the Zodiac was completed").
- Spelling: mars, aberil (twice), may, yong ("absolutely 100%", Koen), iollet, augst, septe(m)b(r), octe(m)bre, nove(m)bre, decebre.
- Koen G's month-name metastudy spreadsheet (https://voynich.ninja/thread-4751.html, 37 visible posts):
  - MarcoP found a near-identical -embre sequence in a Paris(?) book of hours, c. 1348–49.
  - *octembre* = Picard/Walloon "false French made by analogy" (Feller).
  - *aberil* is attested in Switzerland (Glarus, Appenzell "Abrell") and Occitania. eggyk: abrel is commoner west of Augsburg (Swabia, Constance). Catalan "abril" was also suggested.
  - Koen G's current verdict: "almost certainly a peripheral form of French… northern influences". Several posters argue for a mixed French-German writer.
  - Koen lists five stacked unknowns that link these names to the plaintext language.
- The month-name script is being compared with the f116v script, which was dated to central Germany, Fulda/Mainz, with a Zurich outlier (VMD 2025, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4967.html).
- **Status: NEW for provenance, useless as a sign-value anchor**, since a later hand wrote them in Latin script. They are consistent with our negative Romance results but do not bear on them.

**Possible Latin incipit.** In the Pisces thread, Stolfi says he can read "ab aries incipiam" just above the diagram, and nablator confirms "abarieteincipiamus" (thread-4448, March 2026). This is unverified and would be another later Latin-script note. Worth checking on the image.

**Iconography** (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2209.html)
- Taurus' basket is the one fully unparalleled feature.
- The double Cancer is rare, as is the crossbow Sagittarius with its nut behind the trigger. The blue Leo occurs in under 2% of zodiacs, mostly English, 9th–13th century.
- These features point to a German or northern origin, not to a reading.

## 5. Astronomical and cosmological diagrams

**f57v ring** (Anton, Wladimir D, zamolxe; https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-1008.html)
- The 17-glyph sequence repeats 4 times but not exactly. Positions with f appear with p in the 3rd and 4th repeats, and the second-to-last glyph is i-with-crossbar in repeat 1 and e-with-crossbar in repeats 2–4. Repeat 1 may also carry a ":" mark.
- Stolfi (2025) maps the radial guide strokes and wide gaps.
- isa_ou's 531/18 lunar-synodic idea is numerology (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2317.html).
- **EXTENDS our C17/C18 glyph-equivalence work:** f/p swap places inside a fixed sequence.

**f67r2 planets** (oshfdk, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4089.html, 16 pages)
- The label pattern 111101010100 matches a partial domicile chart found in 1490s *De Sphaera* editions, rotated 160°, with Jupiter on top. This gives a positional assignment of 7 labels to planets. *okal* (over 500 occurrences in the manuscript) is the label on top.
- MarcoP passed planet names as a crib to a solver, and 3 Italian names "matched". The thread treats this as weak.
- nablator: Latin diagrams use the genitive (Jovis, Veneris), so short labels can carry only a few letters.
- f67r2's 12 red labels are highly repetitive (ykchy…, ykar ykaly) and can't be 12 sign or month names. geoffreycaveney proposes body parts under the moon's signs (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2672.html).

**f68r** (https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-2258.html, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5915.html, https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2838.html)
- The seven-star group has been read as the Pleiades (Bax's "Taurus"), the Big Dipper or Bootes. A 1409/1419 eclipse has also been proposed.
- A snippet says the Pleiades label was originally *doary* and a later "retracer restored" it as *doaro*. That is a warning that labels on retraced pages are unreliable.
- The star-name decodings (Monica Yokubinas) are unfalsifiable.

**Rosettes foldout**
- Koen G maps dated medieval images of swallowtail merlons, not surviving buildings: Venice c.1310–1324, Naples 1324–31, Genoa 1330–40, Laud Misc. 587 (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3646.html).
- Other comparisons and readings:
  - MarcoP compares the architecture with the Basel 1436 *Notitia Dignitatum* (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3841-post-51520.html).
  - Mark Knowles reads a real route (Milan, Pavia, Bellinzona).
  - ReneZ: the page shows where the healing power of the six central containers comes from.
  - Antonio García Jiménez reads a medieval cosmos (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4204.html).
  - david: "every merlon on the page is swallowtail", so they may be generic (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-92-page-2.html).
- The castle label is unreadable in available images and absent from voynichese.com (https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-3654.html).
- **No text anchor here.**

## 6. Bathing / biological section (Q13)

- **Balneology.** Toresella and Jim Reeds (1996) proposed *De Balneis Puteolanis*. ReneZ's recap is at https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2091.html. Rampling and Clemens mention it in the Yale volume.
  - JKP finds pointy-footed containers and a tight tub in Bodmer 135.
  - Koen G: the vertical lines above the water in Q13B pools best match *De Balneis* imagery.
  - Diane objects that nobody has actually tested the similarity.
- **Allegory.** Rampling: the bathing figures are symbols, with no known alchemical match.
- **Astral influence.** García Jiménez and ReneZ see the tubes as channels for star influence, the same pipes as in the zodiac and the Rosettes.
- **Gynaecology / conception manual** (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5079.html). Koen G objects that the manuscript's scope is far broader and calls it possibly an LLM dump. The social-leadership reading (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5276.html) got no traction.
- Mark Knowles notes that labelling in Q13 is patchy (f76v, f79r/v and f80v are unlabelled; on f80r only the top nymphs have labels).
- **Status: no anchor; nothing testable against our results except label density.**

## 7. Marginalia

**f116v.** Koen G's wiki table of readings (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-710.html):
- poxleber umen; michiton/anchiton oladabas + multos + te + tccr cerc + portas + M; fix + man IX + mor IX + vix + altra + matura (or "marix", "ma+ria"); pals en pbrey so nim geismich o.

Proposed readings:
- Koen G (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4364.html): *poxleber* = billy-goat liver, "so nim" (recipe formula), *gasmich* ≈ goat milk, and charm elements (crosses, Maria, an N for the patient's name).
- *anchiton* appears in Nicolaus de Lyra / the Glossa Ordinaria on Exodus 27, as an incombustible "wood"; Cod. Sang. 266 has "amiton" (Anton, nablator; https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2521.html).
- monisusa (2026) reads a Central Bavarian ointment recipe with *an/c/hi/ton* = "an Zeh hin tun" and *cevc* = quicklime. This is unreviewed (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5838.html).
- Stolfi: "not a single word in that text makes sense, in any language", so a mistaken restoration is the likeliest explanation.
- Script: Fulda/Mainz, early 15th century (thread-4967).
- MarcoP finds word-level parallels in the *Pronostica Galieni* (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2412.html).
- ololololo claims the f116v marginalia contain the Voynichese *aror sheey* (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5880.html).

**f17r.** Readings include "mallier/malher aller lucz her" (JKP: Norse/Norman; Anton: MHG "luch" = marsh; BessAgritianin: *malher althae* = mallow).
- Pelling saw Voynichese at the end of the line under UV, so it was written at the same time as the looped script.
- Stolfi has multispectral histograms (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-498.html, https://www.voynich.ninja/printthread.php?tid=4364&page=4).

**f66r**
- The plain-text "der musdel" was corrected: mel→del, mul→mus, en→den. Anton suggests the corrections were made to stop the marginal text from giving away the code (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-83.html).
- MarcoP and Koen G read f66r and f116v as two "patients plus remedies" scenes by a hand close to the manuscript (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-1970-page-3.html).
- f66r has a left column of single glyphs; f49v has a column of letters with the pattern p/f-o-r-y-e-hook repeated three times, plus modern-looking digits (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-458.html).
- An idea that these are a sieve or masking key is untested (https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-3963.html).

**f1r "AEIOU".** JKP finds an a-e-i-o-u-like glyph run, but says herself "it is not a key" (https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3430.html).

**Status: NEW.** None of these marginalia has given a validated mapping from Voynichese to Latin script. The only Voynichese/Latin mixed lines (f116v, f66r, end of f17r) are short and much disputed.

## TOP 5 FOR US (pre-registrable)

1. **Run Karlov's test on the community's rated herbal-pharma match sheet.**
   - Source: thread-4067, Google Sheet with raters MK, ThP, WD and Koen scoring 0–3. It needs a forum login to get the link.
   - Pre-register:
     - Use only pairs rated ≥2 by at least 2 raters, chosen by drawing alone.
     - Statistic: for each pharma label or f102 panel, the IDF-weighted overlap of glyph-group n-grams (our unit set: ch, sh, iin, ee, qo, dy) with the matched herbal page, against all other herbal pages with the same Currier language and hand.
     - Null: permute the pairings within the same language and hand.
     - Treat fragments on the same panel as one unit.
   - This turns E055, which compared drawings with labels, into the reverse test of drawings against text. A positive result would be the first text-image link not based on a crib.
2. **Test MarcoP's shed-/ched- alternation by sign gender** in the zodiac ring text (thread-580) on ZL3b.
   - Statistic: the share of sh- versus ch- (with -ed- and without) per sign, against the masculine/feminine alternation.
   - Null: all 2^10 gender assignments, or the 252 balanced ones.
   - Controls: run the same test on labels and on Q13 to rule out a page-position drift.
   - If it holds, it is a semantic anchor (sh/ch tied to a known external binary) that constrains sign values. Our C15–C19 machinery for sh/ch can run it directly.
3. **Zodiac label structure, two sharper versions of C22.**
   - (a) Adjacent-sign clustering: a Mantel test of mean label edit distance against sign distance in book order (Pisces → Sagittarius), permuting sign order.
   - (b) takrobat's o- gradient (Aries 97% → Sagittarius 50%): does it follow the sign order, the physical folio order or the hand? Compare it with our q- location effect (C19).
   - If label form drifts with position in the book, as our q- did, the "degree name" reading weakens and the "drifting scribal system" reading strengthens.
4. **Use f57v's 4×17 ring as a natural experiment in glyph equivalence.** The same slot holds f in repeats 1–2 and p in 3–4, and i̵ against e̵ second from the end (thread-1008).
   - Pre-register: if p≡f, as our gallows work suggests and LFD's p/f = elaborate k/t would imply, test whether p/f-initial word pools in running text are distributionally indistinguishable at the same page positions.
   - npcompl33t (thread-6231, Oct 2026) claims p-words follow "fundamentally different construction rules" from k/t words, which directly conflicts with that. We can settle it with our neighbour-distribution test (as for r/l).
5. **Separate C23 into an absolute rate and a ratio, and add the pharma "label-in-own-paragraph" baseline.**
   - MarcoP found about 4 of 200 pharma labels near-matched in their own paragraph, and Stolfi says 88% of label tokens are normal words (12.4% abnormal against 3.7% in text).
   - Report: (i) the exact-type hit rate per page against a shuffled-page null, split by section (pharma, zodiac, Q13, cosmo); (ii) the hapax-label rate.
   - Pre-register f67r2 oshfdk's planet assignment as a held-out positional crib for any future decode. Expected outcome: failure, the same as for every crib in §8. This guards against a false positive.

Not recommended as anchors: month names (later hand, a mixed northern French/Germanic dialect), f116v and f17r readings (contested, probably later or restored), Rosettes castles and Q13 interpretations (no text hook), and single plant-name cribs (none has ever survived).
