## 2. Hypothesis by hypothesis

### 2.1 Latin, plain substitution (including "Vulgar" and "proto-Romance" Latin)
- **Strongest version:** Tim King et al., a Vulgar Latin dialect with Italian influence ([2847](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2847.html); Gioynich: "one of the first papers… with a clear methodology"). Cheshire's "proto-Romance" ([2175](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2175.html), [3341](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3341.html)).
- **Best evidence offered:** a handful of label and word readings.
- **Strongest rebuttal:** MarcoP on Cheshire in 2175. The decipherment reads EVA p as Latin p, but EVA p "almost exclusively appears in the first line of paragraphs. Where are the Ps in the other lines?" It also reads q as d, but q almost always precedes o. Anton posted a sample "translated" passage (*"Molor orqueina doleina dolinar…"*) to show it is not Latin at all. Helmut Winkler: "has not the least to do with Vulgar Latin". Koen G's video ([4365](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4365.html)) says h2 entropy kills every simple substitution: "switching from standard French to any Romance dialect doesn't move the needle at all."
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our negative results for Latin and Italian (searched list) and C2 (h2 below all alphabetic languages). The positional argument (p at paragraph starts, q before o) is a constraint our validated attack already respects implicitly.

### 2.2 Abbreviated Latin, scribal shorthand, Tironian notes
- **Strongest version:** Helmut Winkler, "concatenation of abbreviations" ([4008](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4008.html)). He reads *daiin* as *d' aui* + stroke = *dicit auicenna*, q as *quod*, ch as *caput*, y as *-cum/con* (9). King et al. claim the script is a modified subset of Tironian notes. Also Nicholas G., Maria Rita L. and Ianus van A. on the master list. -JKP- ([3309](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3309.html)): tailed glyphs (r, s, m, g-like) "very frequently represented abbreviations", so word-length arguments may not hold. Bowern & Lindemann (via MarcoP, [3344](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3344.html)) treat EVA m and g as line-final variants of -iin and -y.
- **Strongest rebuttal:** abbreviation goes the wrong way for entropy. Bowern & Lindemann compared abbreviated and full *Secreta Secretorum*: the **abbreviated text has higher entropy** (MarcoP, 3344). Anton (search snippet, thread 2668/148) added abbreviations to Latin and h1 and h2 both *rose*. Only expanding one letter into several glyphs (verbose) lowered them. Members say there is "no evidence that any Voynich characters derive from Tironian notes" (search snippets of 2847/3559). Several snippets also say translations "have no grammar by medieval Latin standards".
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our abbreviated-Latin result and **EXTENDS** it with a mechanism: abbreviation raises h2, while C2 needs h2 much lower. Any abbreviation scheme would therefore need verbose expansion on top.

### 2.3 Romance vernaculars: Occitan, Catalan, Friulian, Italian dialects, Castilian
- **Versions:** the month-name marginalia read as Occitan, Catalan or Judeo-Catalan (thread 4751; this concerns marginalia, not the main text). Friulian as a phonetic transcription of a spoken language (funkylibrarian, [5377](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5377.html), AI-assisted). Castilian (Alisa G.). "Italian shorthand" (Gino C. & Agnese F.).
- **Rebuttal:** ReneZ's simple-substitution detectability experiment ([4930](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4930.html); snippet) correctly rejected Latin, Dutch, German, Polish, Greek, Occitan, Romansh, Catalan and French as plaintexts. MarcoP's reduplication plot ([61 p.9](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-61-page-9.html), read): Old Occitan, Italian, Early Modern Spanish, Middle French, Classical Latin and others have exact back-to-back reduplication about an order of magnitude below Voynichese (about 1%). "The distance is abysmal."
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** C9 and C21 independently with a different corpus (Brian's corpus). Occitan and Catalan were not in our searched list, but the reduplication gap already rules them out under a one-word-equals-one-vord reading.

### 2.4 German (Alemannic or Bavarian)
- **Versions:** Aga Tentakulus (dain = Alemannic "your"; *qo* explained by Alemannic elision, [3475](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3475.html)). Koen G's isogloss project on the f116v marginalia ([3988](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3988.html)). The marginalia are widely accepted as High German. The main text is another matter. tavie noted in 2024 there was "not a single German solution" on the list; one (Thorsten) has been added since.
- **Rebuttal:** German (GMH) sits with the other European languages in MarcoP's reduplication plot. "Das das" and "so so" were floated as German reduplication but do not reach 1%.
- **Relative to us:** **NEW-small.** German is not in our searched list. Low priority, because the reduplication gap applies to it too.

### 2.5 Czech and other Slavic
- **Versions:** Irena H., Elena Konovalova ("technical manual in Old Czech", [3895](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3895.html)), pjburkshire's Old Czech/Moravian ([6221](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-6221.html): "vowels are flexible", "may need to drop the first o"). MarkWart's Czech group ([3932](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3932.html), "modified Old Latin"). A Slavic macaronic idea ([4900](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4900.html), GPT-5-assisted: otal is "kořen" (root), okar is "květ" (flower)). Slovenian (Cvetka) and Russian also appear.
- **Rebuttal:** no intermediate steps are shown. "Vowels are flexible" plus dropping glyphs is the standard degrees-of-freedom failure. Old East Slavic and Czech both fail MarcoP's reduplication comparison.
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our Czech, Slovak, Serbo-Croatian and Ukrainian results. Slovenian and Russian are untested by us but are in the same family profile.

### 2.6 Hebrew
- **Versions:** Rainer Hannig's 60-page paper ([3250](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3250.html); o- = Hebrew article *ha-*). Hauer & Kondrak's anagrammed vowelless Hebrew ([2262](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2262.html), read in full). They decoded line 1 as "She made recommendations to the priest, man of the house…" (that rendering came through Google Translate).
- **Strongest rebuttal (2262):** DonaldFisk ran *his own synthetic fake Voynich text* through Google Translate and got Hindi-detected output: "The tragedy of mourning and hatred of Scott Choke scooters". Point: "it didn't seem to occur to the authors to test their method on text known to be meaningless." -JKP- asked for the A-to-C steps. Hannig's right-to-left direction problem is handled only in a footnote (snippet).
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our Hebrew result and blind-reading word salad. The DonaldFisk control is exactly our "validated attack" philosophy.

### 2.7 Arabic (and other abjads)
- **Versions:** Fletcher C., Mike P. (Arabic with Hebrew and Syriac), the "Andalusi-American" unpointed-Arabic theory ([5405](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5405.html)). geoffreycaveney's general argument ([2667](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2667.html), read): the inventory is too small for vowels and consonants together, so "it must be a vowelless abjad". He cites Stojko's "vowelless Ukrainian" and Book Pahlavi as a defective script that would be unreadable without context.
- **Strongest methodological point:** dfs346 ([4202](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4202.html), read). Correlating 43 ranked glyph frequencies with 43 ranked Arabic letter frequencies gives >90% almost trivially, and "many European languages yield a similar correlation". With an abjad, most vords of up to 3 glyphs will match *some* Arabic root. The test only bites at 4+ glyphs.
- **Relative to us:** **NEW.** Arabic is not in our searched list. Lead: run the validated attack on Arabic, restricted to vords of 4+ glyphs, with shuffle controls. Expected outcome: not found, given C9 and C5.

### 2.8 Turkic
- **Versions:** Ahmet Ardıç et al. (Calgary engineer, [2318](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2318.html); [4682](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4682.html); peer-reviewed papers in a Turkish Cultural Research Institute journal, [5401](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5401.html)). Their evidence is vowel harmony, agglutination, and exact matches "not expected because the author wanted it hard to read". Others: escape (Turkic-Aramaic-Persian, [770](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-770.html)) and Vfind.
- **Rebuttal:** asteckley (5401): "no reasoned out translation of even a limited passage". Rafal (4682): Ardıç transcribes with Turkish letters "which didn't exist around 1400", which steers ChatGPT toward Turkish. joben: "Nationalism… should never be the foundation of a scientific theory."
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our Turkish result. C5 and C14 (the stem barely predicts the ending) are the quantitative counter to the vowel-harmony claim: Turkish suffixes are stem-conditioned and Voynich endings are not.

### 2.9 Romani and Indo-Aryan
- **Versions:** Stephen Bax suggested a non-European language written down in Europe. Gavin Güldenpfennig ([2964](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2964.html): Erromintxela, then a "Romani connection" built on star mythology). Snippets propose "taj" (and) for daiin.
- **Rebuttal:** a 2020 forum test put Romani "out in the wilderness around Turkish, Kurdish, Finnish and Macedonian" (snippet, 1877). There is no historical Romani corpus.
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS** our Romani result.

### 2.10 Chinese and East Asian monosyllabic languages (Stolfi)
- **Strongest version:** Jorge_Stolfi ([4746](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4746.html) and passim). The rigid layered "core-mantle-crust" word grammar plus the narrow, symmetric (binomial) word-length distribution ([3261](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3261.html)) suggest each vord is one syllable of a monosyllabic language written phonetically and not encrypted. Possible languages include Mandarin, Cantonese, Vietnamese, Tibetan, Thai, Burmese, Lao and Hmong. Stolfi in [6238](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-6238.html) (read): A/B could be "two dialects and/or spelling systems (Old vs New Czech, Nynorsk vs Bokmål)" whose digraph statistics differ "as much as two distinct languages". MarcoP got close to Stolfi's word-length shape with Vietnamese VIQR text. In the reduplication plot, **Vietnamese fits Voynich exact reduplication but not quasi-reduplication** (61 p.9, read).
- **Strongest rebuttals:** (a) Historical: who would write it, and why invent an alphabet (dashstofsk, 4746 opening post)? (b) Tones would need marking somewhere (thread-4746 snippets). (c) Stolfi himself, quoted by Torsten in [3257](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3257.html): "the number of different 'words' is far too large compared to the number of syllab[les]". (d) "The syllable structure of Mandarin is simpler and more rigid than Voynichese" (snippet). (e) Stolfi warns that statistics depend on spelling choices: tone marks as diacritics vs a numeric suffix "would radically change the statistics of suffixes" ([5665](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5665.html), read).
- **Relative to us:** **EXTENDS C3** (Mandarin pinyin falls inside the range only with grouping). **Tension with C4:** about 6,000 types and 68% hapax are too many for toneless Mandarin (about 400 syllables, or about 1,300 with tone). Vietnamese orthographic syllables *with tones* number roughly 6–7k, though, so **Vietnamese survives the type-count objection** where Mandarin fails. Stolfi's tone-as-suffix remark also bears directly on C5: a closed set of about 21–28 endings that combine freely with stems is what tone or rime suffixes would look like. **Strongest single match to our profile in the forum.**

### 2.11 Nahuatl and Mesoamerican
- **Versions:** Jacinto Gimenez ([4050](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4050.html)). Tucker & Talbert/Janick's botanical "New World" claim, including a "tl" ligature.
- **Rebuttal:** the carbon date (1404–1438) predates contact. Members say the plant IDs are "clearly way off" (Mark Knowles, 3257).

### 2.12 Constructed and philosophical languages
- **Versions:** Friedman and Tiltman's a-priori language. pfeaster ([4226](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4226.html)) asks how one would even test a conlang. qoltedy ([4978](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4978.html)) proposes a Llull-based a-priori language. Bowern & Lindemann expect a conlang to pattern like natural language morphosyntactically but be odd at the root level; they cite Lingua Ignota, Balaibalan and Enochian. An expert in Koen's video noted that Lingua Ignota maps directly onto Latin syntax. Daruka (2020, Cryptologia) puts Liber Loagaeth tables and VMS sections in "the same linguistic universality class" (snippet; ReneZ: "I cannot recommend this paper").
- **Relative to us:** **NEW and testable.** Our OPEN item lists constructed languages. Liber Loagaeth, the Enochian Calls and Lingua Ignota glossaries all exist digitally.

### 2.13 Glossolalia, automatic writing, gibberish
- **Strongest version:** geoffreycaveney's devil's-advocate post (2667, read). He tried 13 languages (Coptic to Old Prussian) and found any key "comes out far too repetitive, with far too few letters". On the f88r and f99v label rows (oral, oraly, oldar, otoky, otaly) he writes: "an elementary grammar book exercise… as glossolalia, a perfectly logical sequence". Fasano's "Non-Stochastic Glossolalia" (4294). Gaskell & Bowern 2022: 42 volunteers' gibberish was non-random, highly repetitive, and a classifier put most VMS transliterations closer to gibberish (snippets).
- **Strongest rebuttals (4294, read):** Koen G: "positional rigidity… cannot be explained in the context of glossolalia". pfeaster: the glyphs cannot physically be penned at the speed of speech. nablator: line properties prove it is not a transcription of speech; repeated 3-vord sequences are nearly absent in Currier A (27 in ZL). MarcoP: Fasano never engages Gaskell & Bowern.
- **Relative to us:** **CONFIRMS/EXTENDS C9.** Our repeat ratio of 1.0 exceeds Gaskell & Bowern gibberish at 0.66, so naive gibberish under-produces reduplication. Our C12 alternation profile is a new discriminator nobody on the forum has run against the gibberish corpus.

### 2.14 Generators: Timm & Schinner self-citation
- **Version:** Torsten's copy-and-modify model, which reproduces both Zipf laws. The lesswrong article (6238) found lookalike pairs differ at *both* start and end, consistent with copy-and-edit more than with inflection.
- **Rebuttal:** Emma May Smith ([3647](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3647.html), read): "incomplete on an explanatory level". The writer "could have written words with any structure whatsoever, yet consistently kept to the same structure". Koen: a model that has learned the gradient explains the GRU result (npcompl33t in 6238).
- **Relative to us:** our generator ratio of 0.76 on C9 is below the VMS's 1.0. C12 (five specific alternations) is the right test of "small modifications".

### 2.15 Naibbe cipher (magnesium = Michael Greshko; Cryptologia, Nov 2025)
- **Mechanism:** plaintext is split into single letters and bigrams (the 47.2/52.8% split comes from a dice "snake eyes" rule). Each unit is enciphered from one of 6 homophonic tables chosen with playing cards (ratio 5:2:2:2:1:1). The table entries are Voynich words and affixes built on Zattera's slot grammar. It is verbose: a 5-letter word comes out as about 15 glyphs. It generates about 5/6 of Voynich B tokens and was tuned on B only; Latin and Italian worked best ([4848](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4848.html), [5081](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5081.html), read).
- **Author's own stated failures:** no long-range correlations, no line-position mechanism, rare types under-produced (praised by Mark Knowles).
- **Rebuttal:** Torsten ("fatal flaw", circular). The tables and ratios were fitted to Voynich B and then validated on Voynich B. He asks for split-sample validation (fit A, test B) and a separation of fitted from emergent properties. magnesium replies it is a "proof of concept… all models are wrong". Separately (snippet, [5665](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-5665.html)/5718): there is "no memory across word boundaries", while the VMS has the y/l continuation asymmetry and section-dependent k:t ratios. Verbose ciphers in general: single-word labels are a problem, and multi-word repeats would be expected (snippets, [3356](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3356.html)).
- **Relative to us:** **NEW and directly testable.** The 20 reference ciphertexts (Zenodo, Dropbox) and the pykeedy library are public. Prediction from our profile: Naibbe should fail C7/C8 (topic clustering, because letter-level tokens carry little topic), C14 (stem-ending independence is built in, so it may pass), C9 (back-to-back repeats need identical consecutive plaintext units drawn from the same table, so the ratio should be well below 1.0), and C20 (it has no A/B).

### 2.16 Nomenclator and codebook
- Anton ([3956](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3956.html)): a 1:1 codebook of about 8,100 vords needs about 16,200 entries, roughly 25 folios. That is feasible, but 1:1 "would preserve the statistical weirdness". ReneZ's "mod2" toy nomenclator (described by MarcoP in [3107](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3107.html)) assigns consecutive codewords to new words, which yields *quasi*-reduplication. In that scheme, perfect reduplication must come from the plaintext.
- **Relative to us:** **NEW.** It cleanly separates C9 (perfect repeats from the plaintext) from C12 (near-copies from codeword adjacency). It predicts that near-copy pairs cluster at *first occurrences* of words. That is testable on our data.

### 2.17 Glyph groups, bigram = phoneme, syllabic encodings
- Koen G and MarcoP (summarised by geoffreycaveney, 3356): grouping ch, sh, ain, aiin, air, ar, al, am, or, ol, ok, ot, od, qo, qok, qot gives **h1 4.12 / h2 3.01**, inside the *European* range. geoffreycaveney's "bigram = phoneme" ([2668](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-2668.html)): only a couple dozen frequent bigrams.
- **pfeaster's Syllabic Hypothesis** ([4201](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4201.html), opening post read in full). A vord is one syllable (V, CV, VC or CVC) of a *European* plaintext. Spaces mark syllable breaks and are expendable. A syllable vord can span two plaintext words. No plaintext word crosses a line break, which predicts line-start and line-end glyph effects. Writers keep successive syllables in the same shape (CV-CV-CV), producing "a strong tendency towards repetition of similar-looking forms". Empty-slot markers could make one "language" look like another.
- **Relative to us:** **EXTENDS C3** with a tension. We reach simple-syllable languages with a lighter grouping; Koen's heavier grouping reaches European values, so the result depends on the grouping. pfeaster's model matches our profile closely: simple syllables (C3), a closed set of syllable rimes as about 21–28 freely combining "endings" (C5/C14), repetition of similar forms within a line (C9/C21), and loose spelling (C12). The weak point is again **C4**: a Latin text syllabified into about 28.8k syllables probably yields far fewer than 6,000 types unless the encoding is homophonic or variable.

## 3. Consensus methodological lessons (why claims fail)

1. **Degrees of freedom** (Mark Knowles, [3692](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3692.html)): flexible vowels, dropped glyphs, one-to-many sign values and multilingual dictionaries let "any theory fit". If every sign can be three letters, every 4-glyph word has 81 readings (geoffreycaveney, 2667).
2. **Translate a whole paragraph, not words or labels** (geoffreycaveney 2667; asteckley 5401). Solvers "focus almost entirely on the lexicon, ignoring morphology and syntax"; "languages are not word-salads" (Bowern & Lindemann via MarcoP, 3344).
3. **Run the method on known-meaningless text** (DonaldFisk, 2262). Our validated attack already does this.
4. **Respect positional facts:** p and f at paragraph starts, q before o, m and g line-final (MarcoP 2175; 3344).
5. **Zipf, MATTR and frequency correlations are sensitive but not specific** (Stephen Carlson, DonaldFisk, Anton in 3344; dfs346 in 4202). A Markov order-1 process passes Zipf (6238).
6. **Statistics depend on the transliteration and the spelling system** (Mauro and Torsten on Ponnaluri's v101, [4401](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4401.html); Stolfi in 5665; cecilp and MarcoP on h2 in 4294; h2 stays below 2.2–2.5 across 4 transliterations, per ReneZ and Lindemann & Bowern 2021).
7. **Keep fitted and emergent properties apart; validate out of sample** (Torsten on Naibbe, 5081). We already do this with held-out leaves and pre-registration.
8. **Present the generating method, whether the text is meaningful or not** (ReneZ, quoted in [4873](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-4873.html)).
9. **Know the prior work** (ReneZ, 3257): most recent solvers "are unaware of the important statistical properties of the text".
10. **Models trail the discoveries** (Emma May Smith, 3344): each model reproduces what was known when it was built. Hence the push for necessary-criteria batteries (Labyrinthinesecurity, 5665). Stolfi warns those criteria were benchmarked on only 4 languages.
11. **LLM echo chambers** (Rafal, 4682): ChatGPT agrees with the framing it is given. AI-assisted solutions are banned.

## 4. TOP 5 for us (pre-registerable)

1. **Score Naibbe on our full battery (C5/C14, C7/C8, C9/C21, C12, C20).** Use the 20 public reference ciphertexts plus pykeedy runs on Latin and Italian. Pre-register these predictions: C9 repeat ratio well below 1.0; topic clustering (C7) weak; no A/B split. If Naibbe passes C9 and C7, the cipher class becomes our lead hypothesis. If it fails, we have a published, falsifiable cipher architecture ruled out on properties it was *not* fitted to, which is the emergent-property test Torsten asked for.
2. **Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ, tones as a final suffix letter) as the best-matching syllabic language.** It is the only language the forum found that matches Voynich exact reduplication (MarcoP), it survives Stolfi's type-count objection (about 6–7k tonal syllables vs our C4 of about 6,000 types), and tone-as-suffix could produce a closed ending set (C5). Run it through the validated attack with a syllabic-sign model. Pre-register type count, hapax share, ending-inventory size, stem/ending independence and repeat ratio. Corpora: Truyện Kiều, Nôm-era texts in Quốc ngữ, Wikipedia. Tibetan and Thai are secondary.
3. **Test pfeaster's Syllabic Hypothesis on European plaintext.** Syllabify Latin, Italian and German texts, write each syllable as a "word", and optionally add homophonic variants. Measure C3, C4, C5/C14, C9/C21 and line-edge effects. The key go/no-go is whether any syllabic plaintext reaches about 6,000 types per 28.8k tokens without a homophonic layer.
4. **Glossolalic and constructed-language controls.** Run Liber Loagaeth, the Enochian Calls, the Lingua Ignota glossary and the Gaskell & Bowern gibberish corpus through C9, C12 and C14. Our C9 already separates the VMS (1.0) from gibberish (0.66). If C12's five specific alternations also separate them, we will have a discriminator the forum lacks for meaningful vs glossolalic text.
5. **Test the mechanism behind C12 near-copies.** Ask MarcoP's question ([3107](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-3107.html)): are perfect and quasi-reduplication linked? Three models make distinct predictions. ReneZ's mod2 nomenclator predicts near-copies at *first occurrences*. Timm's self-citation predicts near-copies tied to source proximity on the previous lines. Natural spelling variation predicts near-copies independent of first occurrence. Our data can decide among them now.

*Honourable mention:* an Arabic abjad attack restricted to vords of 4+ glyphs, with shuffle controls (dfs346's warning). It is the only major medieval candidate missing from our searched list.
