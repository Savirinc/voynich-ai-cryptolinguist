## Picture so far
Voynich behaves like word-sized units (whole words, or stem + ending) whose common words persist like grammar
words. Within words the glyphs are far more predictable than letters of any alphabet tested, the glyph habits drift
moderately along the book, and the start and end of each line are written differently.

The profile points to a word-level system (a codebook or nomenclator, or a compound stem + ending notation) under
strong scribal line conventions, rather than a letter-by-letter cipher or a simple invented alphabet. Meaning
against human gibberish is still open: the one meaning test we built had no power.

## Proposed Stage B order
1. **B5/compound: word-level codebook or stem + ending notation with a line model.** Known-answer validation first
   (twins with secret codebooks, endings and line rules). Attack: word-level substitution, where Voynich types map
   to plaintext words, solved with a word-level LM plus the E017 confidence rules. Shannon is weaker here (codebooks
   carry more key), so the known-answer test must show crackability at 28k words before Voynich is touched.
2. A meaning test with built-in power: label or repeated-object consistency, checked first on a real labelled
   manuscript text.
3. B1 (learned tables, changing by section) only if 1 fails.
